# VibeBook design guide for generated games

The game templates draw everything: you only choose the ingredients. Make the choices add up to one world that the post's viewer recognises immediately.

## Every genre

- **Title:** 2–3 words, specific to the post. Use the place, the thing or the moment ("Bua Tong Ridge Run", "Azimut Bay Sprint"). Avoid filler words like Dash, Rush, Quest, Adventure and Epic unless one really fits.
- **Tagline:** one concrete picture of what the player does, in plain words, max 90 characters. No hype words (epic, ultimate, insane, legendary) and no exclamation marks.
- **Win line (puzzle, memory):** names what the player rebuilt or found in this post, in one calm line ("Doi Mae U Kho is back in bloom").

## Runner and catch

- **Light:** pick `timeOfDay` from the photo's real light. Golden or pink sky → sunset. Dark sky or city lights → night. Coloured artificial light → neon. Otherwise → day.
- **Cast:** every emoji comes from the photo's own world. In a runner, the player is how someone would move through this place and the obstacles are what is really in the way there. In catch, the catcher is the container people use there, the good items are what the post celebrates, and the bad items are what would spoil it there. Never mix worlds: no sharks on a mountain, no cactus at the beach, unless the post says so.
- **Pace:** match `speed` (and a runner's `density`) to the mood. A calm scenic photo plays chill or normal; action, sport or a crowd plays fast. Use insane only when the post is about speed.
- **Goal:** pick a goal the title promises. Distance for journeys, score for collecting, survive for staying somewhere dangerous.

## Puzzle

- The photo is the whole game, so choose puzzle only when the picture is worth studying.
- Use a 3×3 grid for busy or small-detail photos. Use 4×4 for a bold, simple picture with large shapes.
- Swap mode is friendlier and suits almost everyone. Use slide only when the card promises a classic sliding puzzle.

## Memory

- Pairs are 6–8 different things from this post's world: what you would see, eat, pack or collect there.
- Every emoji must be clearly different at a glance. Never use two flowers or two fruits that look alike.
- Use 6 pairs for a quick round. Use 8 when the post is full of things (a menu, a market, a gear list).
